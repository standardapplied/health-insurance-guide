Why we say this

Self-funded plans cover 67% of workers with employer coverage, usually run by a national administrator under an administrative services agreement. The card carries the administrator’s brand in both arrangements. Pharmacy routing follows the NCPDP card standard; medical claims follow the payer ID.

PART I · ONE VISIT

Seven words decide who pays what.

The premium keeps coverage in force and is paid whether or not care is used. Everything else on this page happens when care is used, in this order: the provider bills a charge, the plan recognizes an allowed amount, and the allowed amount is split between Maya and the plan by her deductible, her coinsurance and her out-of-pocket maximum.

For buildersIn Maya’s plan these seven values, and the running totals behind the deductible and the maximum, decide a single in-network claim. Real plans add copays, tiers, out-of-network rules and exclusions, but each estimate, EOB and remittance still applies rules like these to an allowed amount.

Builder’s detailThe eligibility response (X12 271) carries the deductible and out-of-pocket amounts remaining; the remittance (835) carries the split with adjustment reason codes; the EOB restates it for the member.

Billed charge for this claim: $3,000. The $2,000 difference is written off by the provider; see the bill. Assumes covered, in-network care with no copay and the out-of-pocket maximum unreached.

Premium
What Maya and her employer pay each month for coverage. It never counts toward the limits below.
Allowed amount
The amount the plan recognizes for a service under its contract with the provider. Usually far below the billed charge.
Deductible
What Maya pays first each plan year, in full, until the total reaches $2,000. Then the plan starts sharing.
Coinsurance
After the deductible, Maya’s share of each allowed amount: 20%. The plan pays the other 80%.
Copay
A fixed amount for a visit or a prescription, when a plan uses them. Maya’s plan uses coinsurance alone, to keep the arithmetic in view.
Out-of-pocket maximum
The ceiling on what Maya pays in a plan year, $5,000. After it, the plan pays everything allowed.
Administrator and funder
The administrator runs the plan and prices the claims. The funder pays the plan’s share. In Maya’s plan they are two different organizations.

Rejections, denials and appeals

Rejection

A submission can fail format, identification or other intake checks. The sender may need to correct it before processing can continue.

Denial

A claim decision can deny some or all benefits. The reason and applicable plan procedures determine what happens next.

Correction or appeal

Fixing claim information and challenging a benefit decision are different actions. Follow the plan’s instructions and deadlines.

Why we say this

The explanation of benefits shows the billed amount, the allowed amount, what the plan paid and what the member owes; the remittance to the provider carries the adjustment reasons. Hospitals must now publish their negotiated rates alongside their list charges, which shows the gap directly.

The BlueCard program

Independent Blue companies cooperate when a member receives care outside the Home Plan’s area. The Home Plan issues the policy; the Host Plan is local to the care. In the BCBS Kansas example, the provider submits locally, local pricing applies and the Home Plan decides benefits. Coverage follows the member’s product and the card’s instructions.
